## Verified state flow and authority

The shared context loader reads current position/activity, goal, authored persona and its definition-event anchor (lines 100–125), authored directed relationships, own permitted history and life context (lines 127–137, 172–228, 392–400).
2. Wait initiatives reuse that context loader but set the interlocutor to the session's controlled entity, require a sourced wait completion, and verify the player is currently visible (`backend/internal/storage/rp_initiative.go:44–67`). This is the current player association; it does not prove every private intention or activity targets that person.
3. `readRPOwnPrivateDecisionMemory` reads only committed `RPNPCDecision`/`RPNPCInitiative` proposals with `private`, joined to their actual Event, batch, command and attempt. It checks instance, branch, actor, complete batch visibility at head, other-turn exclusion and approved proposal hash, then returns the latest three in chronological order (`backend/internal/storage/rp_private_decision.go:11–58`). Scope is **actor + instance + branch**, across sessions and interlocutors. It does not filter by current interlocutor.
4. Those rows expose decision/event attribution, source sequence/time and historical interlocutor (`backend/internal/core/rp_decision.go:90–100`). The thought's source Event proves when its associated decision was applied, not that the thought is factual or its goal completed.

6. A three-row SQL result is therefore an upper bound, not a guaranteed three-row provider packet.
7. The external contract requires short private phrases alongside one observable proposal (`backend/internal/decision/chat.go:170–182`; `backend/internal/decision/decision_wire.go:108–112`). Private metadata has text bounds and at most eight distinct authorized source IDs; empty basis is accepted. 8. ## Findings: trigger, impact and confidence

### F1 — Last three does not retain a continuing intent or returning-peer stance

**Trigger:** NPC privately resolves to revisit an offer from A, then makes three private-bearing decisions about other activities or interlocutors before A returns. **Evidence:** SQL sorts the actor's decisions globally and takes three, with no peer/status relevance predicate (`rp_private_decision.go:23–29`). The existing bounded-history test explicitly expects this rolling replacement (`rp_private_decision_test.go:207–229`). Budget selection can remove additional rows as described above.

**Impact:** the private motive/stance disappears from context even though its record remains in storage; the model may reconstruct it from speech or persona, or choose a different position. Public history/life goals mitigate some cases but cannot recover an unspoken private motive. **Confidence:** eviction is verified from code; behavioral forgetting or reversal is an inference, not a reproduced model failure. Increasing the limit postpones eviction and still does not encode lifecycle.

### F2 — Peer attribution is present, but stance applicability is left to the model

**Trigger:** NPC was guarded toward A, becomes warm toward B, then answers A. **Evidence:** all three prior sketches can be supplied, each with historical interlocutor; prompt explicitly says sketches belong to their original time and interlocutor (`chat.go:176`). Schema calls the new stance “Stance toward the current interlocutor” (`decision_wire.go:112`). No deterministic peer selection or stance state exists in `rp_private_decision.go:14–58` / `rp_decision.go:207–212`.

**Impact:** cross-peer stance transfer is possible despite correct actor isolation. **Confidence:** mixed-peer input and model-dependent applicability are verified; actual transfer is inferred. Current attribution is not wrong for supported reactive turns: history obtains the peer from `rp_sessions.controlled_entity_id` (`rp_private_decision.go:16–19`), which matches the enforced speaker. For initiatives this is the player's session association, not an independently typed intention target (`rp_initiative.go:49–52`). A future NPC-to-NPC or actor-only intent cannot safely assume session control identifies its target.

### F3 — Emotional recency has no explicit temporal meaning

**Trigger:** an actor has a distressed sketch, a long world-time wait passes, and no subsequent private-bearing decision replaces it. **Evidence:** the reader caps event sequence, returns world time, but has no freshness/expiry predicate (`rp_private_decision.go:16–29`); the private shape has no validity horizon (`core/rp_decision.go:207–212`). Rows without private do not replace private-bearing rows.

**Impact:** old affect can be treated as current, or disappear arbitrarily after unrelated decisions. Neither three decisions nor session/chapter boundaries specify emotional duration. **Confidence:** missing lifecycle is verified; persistence of any particular emotion is an inference. Do not introduce invented automatic mood decay as a substitute for a product rule. Preserve historical affect with time and an explicit current/unknown distinction.

### F4 — Free text cannot prove fulfillment, abandonment or authoritative relationship change

**Trigger:** private intent says a meeting was completed, or a later sketch stops mentioning an earlier goal. **Evidence:** `RPDecisionPrivate` consists of three phrases and basis IDs; evidence validation checks authorized membership and bounds (`core/rp_proposal.go:18–32`). A source anchor is explicitly not goal completion (`core/rp_decision.go:90–92`). Authored relationship identity/role/address comes from the directed Studio declaration with familiarity checks (`storage/rp_decision.go:416–449`). Sourced social dimensions are a separate deterministic view of observed actions (`core/rp_social.go:65–79`); life context already owns goals and commitments (`core/rp_life.go:73–95`).

**Impact:** treating the newest phrase as a completed promise, reciprocal relationship or trusted fact would manufacture authority. Conversely, interpreting omission as abandonment would lose continuing intent. **Confidence:** structural limitation verified; no current write from these phrases into social canon was found in the audited path. This is a constraint on the proposed design, not evidence that the existing boundary is breached.

## What canon may override

The prompt explicitly orders present trigger/speech, authored readiness/persona/relationships, current scene/own actions, attributed dialogue, permitted knowledge, then earlier private decisions, with canon above model prior (`decision/chat.go:174–180`). Actual world legality, reachability, activity state, controller ownership and head freshness remain server-enforced (`core/rp_proposal.go:33–99`; `storage/rp_decision_commit.go:59–103`). Known authored relations also block self-introduction to that interlocutor (`core/rp_proposal.go:48–52`).

Canon can reject impossible proposed effects and disprove factual premises such as claiming an action completed when its owner has not committed completion. Authored persona and directed role/address constrain portrayal; dynamic sourced social facts inform current stance. They do not directly set an emotion or prove affection, consent or reciprocal trust. The actor may privately be conflicted about a canonical relation. Speech proves utterance, not truth; an old private stance proves earlier model metadata, not current stance. Missing persona/relationship stays MISSING/UNKNOWN rather than importing famous-character lore. Rich semantic consistency remains a prompt obligation, not a universal deterministic canon validator.

## Smallest durable flow, using the existing ledger

**First increment, no new world store:** retain the last-three chronology as recent history, and deterministically select a separately attributed prior stance for the **current directed actor/peer pair** from the same committed, hash-checked rows at the current head. Also select the actor's latest affect with original time, as historical affect rather than guaranteed current mood. Keep peer filtering before the limit; never search another actor's private record. Label incomplete/missing state explicitly and count selection omissions. This improves return-peer continuity but does not claim durable unresolved-intent semantics.

**Minimum for actual long-lived intent:** add a versioned, bounded private state-update shape inside the existing approved proposal ledger. Give continuing intent a stable actor-owned ID, explicit scope (`actor` or directed `actor_peer`), and a small lifecycle (`retain`, `replace`, `abandon`; add observed completion only when an existing typed owner supplies a matching sourced outcome). Bind every update to prior decision/state revision, current head, source decision, world time and optional target. Separate intent lifecycle from latest affect and directed stance so incidental emotion changes cannot delete a plan. Optional expiry must be a declared world-time rule. Do not deduce completion or cancellation from a prose phrase or absence.

The existing decision owner validates target/scope, predecessor and references, then persists the private update **only in the atomically applied decision row**, alongside its observable batch. At the next input, a deterministic fold over authorized immutable rows reconstructs a bounded current private view; any cached result is disposable/rebuildable, never independent world truth. No write during prompt generation, audit-only proposal, rejected candidate, provider retry, cooldown or fallback should advance private lifecycle without an applied explicit update. Selection should reserve a small bounded slot for current private state or visibly report inability to include it. The provider can propose changes but cannot directly edit canon, world goals, social relations or commitments. Keep facts and goal outcomes in their current typed owners/ledgers.

This is a design recommendation, not an implemented migration or plan edit. Explicit actor/peer binding belongs in the private approved record for future paths; existing supported rows may be read using their proven reactive/session binding, but old sketches should not be retroactively classified into long-lived intents by an unverified summarizer.

## Compatibility, privacy and recovery constraints

- **Compatibility:** private is optional in internal proposals and old deterministic rows may omit it. Preserve legacy rows/hashes exactly; read a versioned new shape without rewriting immutable JSON. Historical three phrases remain history, not automatically active intent. A new input field changes context/input hashes; preserve replay paths and version packet/selection semantics. Do not upgrade an already staged decision against a new input contract silently.
- **Privacy:** include the folded view only in this actor's private decision input and authorized restricted diagnostics. Reuse unfamiliar-ID masking. Keep it out of Events, narration, participant outbox, Play observation and public observatory DTOs; consider mixed-peer phrases and arbitrary free text separately from structural ID masking. Controller transfer must preserve existing authority checks and must not automatically expose prior NPC private content to the new human/controller.
- **Recovery:** the ledger currently survives local restart/rebuild according to the existing test assertions (`rp_private_decision_test.go:190–203`, inspected but not executed). Private content is intentionally absent from Events, so an Event-only export/reconstruction cannot restore it. Include immutable approved decision rows, sessions needed for legacy peer attribution, command/attempt hashes and batch bindings in any private-state recovery contract. Missing private ledger means unknown/degraded continuity, not fabricated state. Fork/copy semantics were not established by this audit; a future fold must not accidentally share child/parent branch private records.
- **Concurrency:** use existing head/hash and internal-owner checks for both reactive and initiative updates; deterministic IDs/revisions make retry idempotent. Never update a private cache before commit succeeds. Rebuild should derive exactly the same fold and input hash from the same ledger/head; corrupted selected row hashes already fail closed (`rp_private_decision.go:41–47`).
- **Boundedness:** a global last-N scan cannot guarantee retention of active intents. Fold once into a disposable index/checkpoint with verifiable ledger anchors if history becomes expensive; cap active intent count/phrase length and explicitly terminate or reject overflow rather than evicting an active intent silently. Such an index is a projection of the private ledger, not a second authoritative world state.

## Verification and finite follow-up

### Critique of a sparse transition contract

An explicit `keep/start/resolve/abandon` contract is appropriate for continuing intent if `resolve` is narrowly defined. `resolve` must not imply a world outcome unless its current typed owner committed the required evidence; if it means only “the actor no longer pursues this,” distinguish that private closure from objective fulfillment. Avoid requiring the model to restate every active intent on every reply. Missing transition (including every legacy private sketch and provider fallback) should mean **no update**, not reset, keep-with-new-basis, expiry or completion. Explicit `keep` is useful only for a deliberate revision of rationale/validity and can otherwise be omitted.

Concrete minimum: a bounded optional `intent_updates_v1` list in the approved private proposal, with operation, a packet-local existing-intent handle for keep/resolve/abandon, bounded phrase only on start or deliberate revision, declared actor/actor-peer scope and target handle, and eligible evidence refs. On start, the owner binds the durable ID to `(instance, branch, actor, committed decision ID, update ordinal)`; the model does not create arbitrary durable IDs. Existing-intent handles resolve only to this actor's active state in the actual packet. No transfer to another actor, no arbitrary target reassignment and no absent “previous” intent accepted. Changing a peer scope is abandon + start. Retaining A's intent while speaking with B is allowed when explicitly scoped to A; it must not become B's stance or knowledge. A small explicit active-intent bound makes overflow a validation result, not silent eviction.

Reduce only rows passing the current reader's full committed command/attempt, approved proposal hash, actor, instance/branch and **batch.last_sequence <= requested head** predicates (`rp_private_decision.go:18–28`). A snapshot at the first Event of a multi-Event batch must not see the transition until the complete batch is visible. Chronological Event order plus update ordinal produces deterministic ordering; validate all updates against the incoming revision and apply the list atomically. Reactive and initiative owners both commit through their existing transaction/idempotency paths. No side state write from provider calls is necessary.

Sparse history can be streamed into a bounded active map: memory is bounded by active-intent count, but read cost grows with every historical transition and cannot be limited to last N without losing still-active intent. It is the simplest initial implementation for modest history. A full checkpoint on every reply repeats unchanged private text, changes hash volume, and risks hiding missed transition validation; it is not required for correctness. When measured replay cost warrants it, use a disposable private projection/checkpoint with reducer version, instance/branch/actor, complete-batch sequence and validated ledger anchor. Replay remaining eligible updates after that cursor. Drop/rebuild it on incompatible reducer or invalid anchor; never use a future-head checkpoint for an earlier snapshot. Do not treat mutable projection contents as evidence or as an independently editable current goal store.

Restart with no cache replays the retained private decision ledger and produces the same active state. Retry returns the existing committed decision before applying any transition again. Projection failure must not publish partially folded state: recover by replay or explicitly report unavailable private continuity. The approved private ledger remains necessary recovery material because Events intentionally exclude it. This extension is confined to private intent continuity; existing `Life.Goals`, commitments, social dimensions and authoritative action completion stay in their owners. Peer-specific historical stance retrieval remains the cheaper first increment when the product only needs stance recall rather than long-lived intent lifecycle.

Evidence is source inspection, not a live provider experiment. Existing tests cover actor/instance/branch/head isolation, public privacy, audit-only exclusion, rebuild/restart stability and chronological rolling bounds (`rp_private_decision_test.go:104–105,130–135,157–203,207–229`); the initiative privacy test covers retained private rows without observable Event leakage (lines 14–74). These were not rerun, and they do not establish semantic character continuity.

For a future implementation, focused deterministic cases should cover A→B→A after more than three applied private decisions; unresolved intent retained through incidental replies; explicit abandonment/replacement; historical affect after elapsed time; no private-state advancement on rejected/staged/replayed/fallback attempts; real completion requiring its typed owner's source; actor/branch/controller boundaries; and identical folded input after restart/rebuild. No full suite is needed merely to validate this audit document.
